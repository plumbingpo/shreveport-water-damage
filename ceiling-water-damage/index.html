Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Shreveport

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. Common sources are the roof, an air conditioning drain, a pipe running overhead, or the bathroom one story up. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
Repair or Replace

Dry It or Replace It?

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Water Travels Sideways

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

Electricity Overhead

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Secure the Room

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.
