Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Shreveport

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Shreveport, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Adjacent Room Flooring

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Shared Garage Wall

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • If water got to the flooring of adjoining rooms, and the kind of flooring involved.
  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
  • The length of time the tank went on refilling until someone closed the supply valve.
  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.
