The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. In Shreveport, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. They are two phases. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's what you can expect when you choose Shreveport Restoration:

1

Control the Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

Why Professional Water Damage Mitigation Matters

Mitigation is a series of judgment calls made quickly. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? Our team makes these calls from training and from moisture readings, not from how a surface looks or feels to the hand.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.

The hands-on work of stopping the source, removing water and setting containment is often finished on the first visit. Drying then usually takes 3 to 5 days, with our team checking moisture readings along the way. Contaminated water, bigger losses and dense materials such as plaster or hardwood may extend that. Most families remain at home. Barriers separate the work area, and although the equipment is warm and noisy, running it nonstop is safe. Category 3 water, a kitchen or sole bathroom out of service, or a household member with breathing trouble are reasons to consider staying somewhere else. That conversation happens on the first day.
Fixing the pipe stops new water, but it does nothing for the water already in the house. Wet drywall, flooring and cabinets keep absorbing moisture until it is extracted and dried. In damp materials, mold may start in 24 to 48 hours. A meter reading is wise when the water reached anything other than hard flooring.
Stopping the spread of damage is what mitigation does. Source control, extraction, containment and stabilization that lets the home dry all fall under it. Next is restoration, which puts the home back as it was with repairs like new flooring, drywall and paint. Mitigation is urgent and measured in days. Scheduled restoration work may run longer based on the extent of damage.
Certain materials trap moisture that equipment alone will not pull out. Carpet pad, swollen baseboard and crumbling drywall are common examples. If they stay, the subfloor and framing remain damp and the whole job dries more slowly. Removal decisions come from meter readings, and each removed item is listed for your insurance file.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
