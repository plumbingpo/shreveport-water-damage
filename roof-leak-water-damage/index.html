Roof Leak Water Damage in Shreveport: What to Know

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Catch the DripsPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. From indoors, mark where the water enters, and let professionals handle everything else.
  5. Document What You SeeBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • Clogged gutters that back water up under the shingles at the roof edge.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Mold in the Attic

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

Drywall Ceilings

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Insurance

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • How much attic insulation is wet and whether it has to be removed.
  • How easy the attic is to reach, how high the ceilings are, and if the roof needs a tarp before work begins.
  • The amount of ceiling and wall drywall that needs to be cut out.
