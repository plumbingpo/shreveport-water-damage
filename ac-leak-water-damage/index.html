AC Leak Water Damage Help in Shreveport

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Failure of the small pump that lifts condensate to a drain.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.
