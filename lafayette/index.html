Restoration Challenges Lafayette Homeowners Face

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Lafayette, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. Our Lafayette technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Lafayette.

Simple Ways to Keep a Lafayette Home Dry

Most of the water damage we see in Lafayette homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Start with supply lines. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. If you find something in your Lafayette home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.

What to Do Before Our Lafayette Crew Arrives

People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. These are the mistakes our crews see most often in Lafayette, and what to do instead.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Leave ceiling fixtures off if the ceiling above is wet, and keep away from any part that droops. Leave fitted carpet in place, because pulling it up the wrong way can stretch or rip a carpet we might have saved. Avoid cranking up the heating, as warmth plus moisture encourages mold.

The helpful things are simple: close the valve, prop open cupboards and wardrobes, and stand wet cushions and textiles up to drain. Photograph each room before you shift a single item. Keep kids and animals out until it has been checked. Most of all, phone early, so a Shreveport Restoration crew is already heading for Lafayette while you work through this list.

Drying Out a Lafayette Property: What to Expect

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. Carpet can be dry on top while the pad is soaked, and painted drywall can look fine while the back of it is wet. For that reason we rely on meters, not on feel. Our Lafayette technicians carry probe meters for wood and wallboard, scanning meters that read through finishes, and infrared cameras that pick up damp areas as cooler spots.

Not everything dries at the same rate. Carpet and drywall respond quickly, while framing lumber, subfloor, hardwood and concrete hold on much longer. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. Most jobs finish in 3 to 5 days, and if yours will take longer you will hear it from us at the start.

The expensive error is pulling equipment too soon. Damp materials sealed behind new finishes may mold, buckle or delaminate months afterward. Every Lafayette job is therefore dried to a recorded target, and the log stays on file.
