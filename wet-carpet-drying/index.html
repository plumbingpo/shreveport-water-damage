Wet Carpet Drying: What Shreveport Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. Our team in Shreveport extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • A rust colored line at the wall edge, a sign of wet tack strip
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Furniture Marks

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Mold Under the Carpet

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Water Held in the Pad

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

What We Do

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
