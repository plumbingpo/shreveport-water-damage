Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Shreveport

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Shreveport crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Bases and Trim

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Kitchen Cabinets

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Whether the water was clean supply water or gray discharge that needs sanitizing.
  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
