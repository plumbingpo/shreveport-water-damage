Filing an insurance claim for water damage can be overwhelming, especially when you are already dealing with a flooded or damaged property. Damage That Is Often Covered

Most standard homeowner's insurance policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental. That category takes in failed appliances like water heaters, dishwashers, and washing machines, along with sudden HVAC failures, accidental tub or sink overflows, ice dams, and burst pipes. Coverage typically extends to water extraction, structural drying, material replacement, and related restoration costs, including temporary living expenses if your home is uninhabitable.

What's Typically NOT Covered

Several kinds of loss are normally left out: groundwater seepage at the foundation, long-term leaks, problems caused by putting off maintenance, floods from external sources (a separate flood policy is needed), and sewer backups (a specific endorsement is needed). Understanding these exclusions before a loss occurs lets you add endorsements or separate policies to fill coverage gaps.

Steps to File a Successful Claim

To keep your claim protected, begin by documenting the damage thoroughly, with pictures and video, while nothing has been cleaned up yet. Report the loss to your insurer as soon as you can. Take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage, which insurers call mitigation. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has inspected them. Hire a restoration company that holds IICRC certification. Save every receipt tied to the loss, from temporary repairs to living expenses. When the adjuster visits, be there to walk them through all of the damage.
